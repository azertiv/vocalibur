Transparency

What we do before writing “research shows”.

Version of October 26, 2026Vocalibur editorial team, Pengram

Vocalibur’s articles serve two purposes: helping learners understand memory and documenting the teaching choices behind the product. They must not turn a single study into a sales promise. This page makes the method checkable and sets out the rules for corrections.

1. We start from a useful question, not an empty keyword

A topic is chosen when it answers a real decision: how many new words to introduce, why a card comes back, what a vocabulary test measures or how to read an estimate. Search optimization helps us phrase the question in the reader’s own words; it justifies neither a redundant article nor a mass-produced page.

2. We look for the source closest to the result

A meta-analysis, the original paper, the official CEFR text or the model’s documentation come before a blog post that summarizes them. A secondary source can give context, but it never carries a central figure on its own. References are listed at the end of each article, and links lead as far as possible to the journal, the DOI or the institutional repository.

3. We keep the conditions of the study

Population, material, type of test, interval and final measure determine what a result means. An advantage observed in one spacing condition doesn’t become “64% more memory for everyone”. A number of word families linked to text coverage doesn’t become a CEFR level. A table of guided hours doesn’t become a personal date.

4. We separate four levels of evidence

Experimental result: what a study observed. Synthesis: what several studies allow us to generalize, with caution. Vocalibur choice: a design or product decision consistent with these results, without being “proven” by them. Hypothesis: an expected effect that still has to be measured in the app.

5. We name the limits

The CEFR is not a word counter. A short, uncalibrated test doesn’t certify a level. FSRS estimates a probability from a history; it doesn’t know the exact moment of forgetting. Laboratory studies don’t reproduce the full length and variety of real learning. These limits appear next to the conclusion they concern, not in a note nobody can find.

6. Writing assistance is not an authority

This library was written and reviewed with AI assistance. This work is neither independent scientific expertise nor a review by a native-speaking human. When only the abstract of a study is available, the article doesn’t attribute to it details that the abstract doesn’t contain. Editorial approval for publication remains a separate step, under the publisher’s responsibility.

7. Corrections stay visible in the process

Each article shows its review date. At each publication, the site build rejects forbidden promises, incomplete sources and broken links. This check doesn’t replace reading: each article is reread against its sources and against the app. Significant corrections are recorded in the repository before publication.

What we will not publish

No fake “best app” rankings written by the publisher, no invented testimonials, no guaranteed individual results, no thousands of near-identical programmatic pages and no sources added after the fact to decorate a claim. If a piece of Vocalibur data doesn’t exist yet, it is presented as a hypothesis or left out.

Report an error

Does a source fail to support a sentence, is a linguistic term ambiguous or is a link broken? Write to contact@pengram.app, naming the article and the passage. Correcting the text takes priority over defending it.
