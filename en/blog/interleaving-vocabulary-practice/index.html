Interleaving: should you mix up your vocabulary?

3 min readReviewed October 26, 2026Method and corrections

Translated and adapted from French by the Vocalibur team. Sources are cited in their original language.

Key points

  • Interleaving doesn’t have the same advantage in every domain.
  • A comparison between confused words should spell out their differences.
  • A varied queue of cards is not automatically a validated interleaving procedure.

Mixing up exercises can help you choose the right answer instead of automatically reusing the method from the previous exercise. But mixing up English words doesn’t necessarily improve how well you learn them. The effects of interleaving vary with what you are learning, and it is best to say so from the start.

Two arrangements that are easy to tell apart

Blocked practice can follow the order AAABBBCCC. Interleaved practice can follow the order ABCABCABC. The letters stand for categories of exercises, not levels of difficulty. Both sequences contain the same number of items; only their order changes.

In geometry exercises, a block implicitly announces the procedure: after several calculations of a cylinder’s volume, the next one will probably need the same formula. In a mixed series, you first have to recognize which procedure applies. That difference matters if the final test asks you to make exactly that choice.

Spacing, on the other hand, means separating your encounters with the same material in time. Mixing can create spacing, but the two manipulations are not the same. An experiment has to control for both if it wants to attribute a result specifically to the order of the exercises.

Solid results in mathematics, under defined conditions

Rohrer and colleagues (2020) ran a preregistered trial, with random assignment by class. In fifty-four seventh-grade math classes in the United States, students did their homework either in blocks or interleaved for four months, followed by a shared review. The delayed test favored interleaved practice.

This design provides evidence closer to ordinary teaching than a short, isolated laboratory task. But the randomization is by class, and the result only holds for the problems studied: this study doesn’t make interleaving a rule for all the material in an app.

For words, the picture is different

The meta-analysis by Brunmair and Richter (2019) brings together 59 studies. The average effect favors interleaving, but it varies with the material: results are favorable for some visual categorization tasks and for mathematics, while the subset based on words favors blocked practice on average. The authors explicitly recommend caution for words and expository texts.

A subgroup average doesn’t mean that mixing harms every learner. It is enough, however, to rule out a promise such as “every mixed list helps you remember better”. The similarity of the categories, the task and prior knowledge all need to be taken into account.

A targeted comparison can still be useful

If your difficulty is with borrow and lend, putting two sentences side by side can help you see who lends and who borrows. If you confuse price and prize, compare the meaning and the form explicitly. This work responds to a confusion you have identified; it is not the same as randomly mixing a hundred unrelated words.

You can then hide the solutions and decide which word fits a new sentence. For example: The winner received a ___. The meaning needed is a reward, so prize. For The ___ includes delivery, it is the amount to pay, so price. These sentences were written for this exercise.

Choose the order according to your goal

When you are discovering a word, a stable, clear context can help. When you need to tell apart uses you have already met, a comparison can become relevant. To check that you can choose without the chapter giving away the answer, a series mixing several categories can be informative.

Look at what your mistakes tell you. If you keep mixing up meanings, go back to the explanation. If your answers are only correct in an order you have memorized, change the order next time. These are leads to check against your own answers.

What mixing means in Vocalibur

A review queue can bring together words from several topics because they fall due on the same day. That describes how the session is organized. It doesn’t prove that the algorithm carries out the interleaving procedure of a study, or that it always avoids every confusion between words.

In Vocalibur, use the sentences to understand the expected answer, and let the schedule organize the returns. If one word reminds you of another, compare how they are used. What counts is still your ability to tell them apart in a new situation, not how jumbled the list is.

Frequently asked questions

Is mixing all topics always more effective?
No. The review cited finds different results depending on the material, with an average result that favors blocked practice for words. Mixing should respond to a specific difficulty.
When should you compare two similar words?
When you need to tell their uses apart. Start with clear examples, then check your choice in a new sentence. This is a suggested exercise, not a universal rule.
