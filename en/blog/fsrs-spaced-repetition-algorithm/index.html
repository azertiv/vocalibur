Method

FSRS: how the next review is calculated

4 min readReviewed October 26, 2026Method and corrections

Translated and adapted from French by the Vocalibur team. Sources are cited in their original language.

Key points

  • FSRS estimates a probability from a history; it doesn’t read your memory.
  • The algorithm and the way an app builds it in need to be kept apart.
  • A better forecasting score doesn’t, on its own, show a percentage of time saved.

Why does one card come back tomorrow and another in several weeks? A spaced repetition scheduler turns a history of answers into upcoming due dates. FSRS is a family of open models used for this calculation. It estimates probabilities; it doesn’t directly measure what your brain has stored.

From SM-2 to a forecasting model

The original description of SM-2 by Piotr Woźniak dates the first versions of the program to 1987. The algorithm adjusts the intervals and an ease factor according to the quality of the answers. So it would be inaccurate to describe it as one identical schedule for every card.

FSRS represents the state of a card differently and makes it possible to fit the model’s parameters to review histories. You need to tell apart the published algorithm, its version and how it is built into an app. An optimization feature available in Anki is not automatically available in every piece of software that uses FSRS.

Three ideas for reading the estimates

The FSRS-6 documentation defines difficulty, stability and retrievability. Difficulty changes according to your answers: it is not an objective CEFR level for the word. Stability is the interval at which the model predicts 90% recall. Retrievability is the estimated probability of recall at a given moment.

So a stability of thirty days doesn’t mean the word will stay intact for thirty days before it starts to fade. It only means that, thirty days after the review, the model forecasts a 90% chance of recall. FSRS-6 uses 21 parameters; their values and how they are fitted depend on the implementation. These statistical variables shouldn’t be taken literally as biological quantities.

Target retention is a trade-off

The Anki manual explains that a higher retention target generally leads to shorter intervals and more reviews. Getting close to 100% can cost a lot of work for a limited gain. The target guides a forecast: it doesn’t guarantee the outcome of each of your answers.

An app also has to decide on its relearning steps, its daily limits and how to convert your swipes into grades. These choices change the experience. Saying only “it uses FSRS” isn’t enough to describe everything the app actually does.

What a benchmark shows, and what it doesn’t

The srs-benchmark compares the forecasts of several models on review histories. It keeps training data separate from evaluation data and uses, among other measures, log loss, which penalizes probabilities that fit the observed answers badly. The procedure and the ranking change over time; a figure should always be tied to its version.

Forecasting recall better is an interesting result. FSRS aims for 20 to 30% fewer reviews than the classic SM-2 algorithm, at comparable retention; that is the model’s goal, not a gain measured in Vocalibur or guaranteed to everyone. To establish such a gain, you would need to define a scheduling policy and compare the workload against retention that is actually measured, for example in a suitable prospective experiment.

So we promise neither a precise amount of time saved for everyone nor “the best algorithm available”. Histories can be selected, people grade in different ways and the answers observed only cover part of what it means to know a word.

Why the quality of your answer matters

Imagine you didn’t retrieve the word, but you then mark it as a success because the solution seems obvious. The scheduler receives information that differs from what really happened. On the other hand, treating every hesitation as complete forgetting can underestimate what you had retrieved.

Good practice is to answer as the app asks, separating what you knew before revealing the card from what became clear afterwards. In Vocalibur, “Reveal” is there for checking and has no effect on the algorithm: only your answer counts, “I know it” to the right or “To review” to the left. “Mastered” (swipe up), Archive (swipe down) and “Already known” (the very first right swipe on a word you have never seen, an option that is on by default) take the word out of scheduled reviews: these are choices you declare, not ordinary learning answers.

What you can reasonably expect from Vocalibur

Vocalibur uses FSRS-6 to adapt due dates to the answers it records. This adaptation doesn’t mean the general parameters are continuously retrained on your history, or that the app sees what you read elsewhere.

In this version of Vocalibur, the schedule uses the standard FSRS-6 parameters: the model’s general settings. The state and history of each card still change with your answers. With Premium, a personal memory model, fitted to your reviews, can be viewed in Settings; it isn’t used to calculate due dates.

If the workload grows, look at the number of new words and the reviews that have piled up as well. If a card stays difficult, look for a confusion of meaning or a missing explanation. An algorithm can choose a due date; it doesn’t automatically make an example understandable.

FSRS is therefore an organizing tool. It handles a repetitive calculation so you can work on the words, but checking meaning, practicing in real situations and choosing a pace that fits your schedule remain up to you.

Frequently asked questions

Does FSRS know when I will forget?
No. It produces a statistical estimate. A next due date doesn’t point to an exact biological moment.
Does Vocalibur keep retraining the model on my answers?
No. Vocalibur schedules with the standard FSRS-6 parameters, the same for everyone. Your answers change the state of each card, and therefore its due dates. With Premium, a personal memory model, fitted to your reviews, can be viewed in Settings; it doesn’t calculate any due dates.
